VELVET / 01 RESIDENTIAL

THE MONEY
NEVER LEFT.

Velvet is what happens when old wealth survives long enough to become part of the infrastructure.

The mansions stayed. The estates became institutions. Family businesses became businesses that still had the same family portraits hanging behind the register. Expensive buildings accumulated damp, repairs accumulated behind repairs, and entire properties were inherited by people who had no fucking idea what half the rooms were for.

People still live here. People still work here. People get married here, go to school here, eat dinner here, run errands here, inherit houses here and complain about property taxes.

Velvet is not abandoned.
It is inhabited.

VELVET / 02 ORDINARY LIFE

Velvet has grocery stores. Restaurants. Delivery drivers. Schools. Tailors. Mechanics. Private clubs. Funeral homes. Antique shops. Lawyers. Doctors. Landscapers. People walking their dogs through streets lined with buildings older than entire neighborhoods elsewhere.

The district looks expensive until you notice the water stains.

The stonework is maintained because somebody's grandfather insisted it be maintained. The roof is held together because somebody's cousin knows a guy. The chandelier works because three different generations of electricians have apparently agreed never to ask why the wiring goes where it does.

INSIDE THE HOUSES

EXPENSIVE
DECAY.

Velvet interiors are full of the same contradiction as the streets outside them.

Imported wallpaper peeling away from plaster. Dark wood polished around areas where the floor has started to sink. Antique furniture nobody is allowed to throw away. Carpets that have survived several owners and at least one flood.

There are portraits everywhere.

Some of them have been facing the same wall for eighty years. Some haven't.

THE BALLROOMS

SOME ROOMS
ARE STILL IN USE.

Others are technically abandoned.

This distinction gets less useful the longer you live here.

A ballroom can be closed for forty years and still have fresh flowers in it. A family can stop holding ceremonies there and still leave offerings at the same place every year.

Nobody agrees on who started doing it.

ESTATES / GARDENS

THE OUTSIDE
ISN'T BETTER.

The gardens are enormous.

Old hedges, dead roses, fountains, statues, iron fencing and paths designed by people who apparently had enough money to make walking unnecessarily complicated.

Some gardens have been reclaimed by weeds. Some are meticulously maintained. Some have both happening at once.

Weddings happen here.

So do other things.

VELVET / 04

THIS IS
WHERE IT GETS
FUCKING WEIRD.

Velvet's supernatural shit doesn't announce itself with a thunderclap.

It behaves like an old family custom.

A locked room. A bowl of offerings. A staircase nobody remembers building. A ceremony that appears in the household calendar every thirteen years. A beautiful thing standing in the hallway that looks almost human until you realize its reflection is doing something else.

Some residents call these traditions. Some call them superstition. Some know exactly what they're doing.

The district has been here for a very long time.
So have some of its guests.

NO OFFICIAL CLASSIFICATION

BEAUTIFUL.
PROBABLY.

There are things in Velvet that look like they belong here.

That's the problem.

Old formal clothing. Human proportions. Polite posture. Familiar faces that aren't attached to anyone you know.

You can spend an entire evening speaking to something before noticing that it has never blinked.

OUTLIER / MOTHBALL

DOLL
HATES THIS PLACE.

Doll comes from Mothball, the cramped secondhand-commerce enclave inside Velvet.

She knows the district well enough to navigate its expensive buildings without giving them the respect they apparently think they deserve.

She has a battered embroidered handbag. It contains things nobody should ask about.

She looks at an enormous ancestral mansion the way most people look at a parking ticket.
