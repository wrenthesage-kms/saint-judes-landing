THE CITY
KEPT ADDING
ROOMS.

Mothball grew inside Velvet instead of beside it. Storefronts occupied old ground floors. Apartments got divided. Basements became storage. Storage became shops. Shops expanded into hallways. Somebody added a staircase. Somebody else forgot why it was there.

Eventually the whole thing became a dense little ecosystem of secondhand commerce, salvage, repair, curios, collectors and objects changing hands so frequently that nobody remembers where half of them came from.

ABOVE THE STORES

PEOPLE
LIVE HERE.

Families live upstairs. Renters live above storefronts. Kids grow up hearing shop doors slam below them. Laundry hangs over alleys. TVs bleed through walls. Inventory gets shoved into hallways because there isn't anywhere else to put it.

In Mothball, the boundary between home and workplace is fucking theoretical.

SOMEONE WILL USE IT

DON'T
THROW
IT OUT.

Almost everything has another use, another owner or another story. Sometimes it's valuable. Sometimes it's useful. Sometimes it's ugly as sin.

Sometimes nobody knows where it came from anymore. That does not necessarily mean it belongs in the garbage.

AFTER DARK

THE SHOPS
CLOSE.

Not all of them.

People still live here. Somebody is always running a late-night shop. Somebody is dragging furniture through an alley at midnight. Somebody upstairs has left the lights on.

Old buildings glow through dirty windows and Mothball keeps doing what it does.

UNDER THE STORES

THE BASEMENT
IS ALWAYS
BIGGER.

Storage becomes basement. Basement becomes older basement. Older basement becomes foundation.

Some foundations predate the building currently sitting on top of them. Some spaces are too large to make sense.

Nobody in Mothball is particularly surprised anymore.

MAYBE THIS WAS ALWAYS HERE

THAT DOOR
WASN'T THERE
YESTERDAY.

Or maybe it was.

Mothball is difficult to remember accurately. Merchandise moves. Businesses renovate. Storage gets rearranged. Buildings are divided and subdivided. Old architecture gets covered up and uncovered again.

A door appearing where nobody remembers a door being is not necessarily enough to stop business.

THERE IS
NO WALL.

There is no gate into Mothball.

The architecture just gets tighter. Storefronts get closer together. Signs multiply. Upper floors become visible. Merchandise starts appearing outside.

Expensive old Velvet architecture gets swallowed by commerce, then eventually gives way to the wider district again.
